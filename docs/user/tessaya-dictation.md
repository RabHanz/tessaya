# Dictation

Turn speech into text in any app.

## Start

Hold **Alt+Win**, speak, release. The text is typed where your cursor is. **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Space** toggles it on and off instead of holding. The keys appear after the speech model has downloaded (Voice page, or tick Always listening). **Double-tap** the hold key (two quick taps) and Tessaya keeps listening after you let go; press the key once more to finish. While you hold the key the pill has no ✓ or ✕: letting go inserts, and pressing Esc before you let go throws it away. With the toggle key or a double-tap the pill stays up with ✓ and ✕. Turn it off on the Dictation page (Double-tap to keep talking). To dictate by voice, say "dictate". The pill stays up while you speak, names the window your words are for and shows the last few of them; nothing is typed yet. Say "stop dictation" or click ✓ on the pill to insert it all into that window, or click ✕ or press Esc to throw it away. Her name and "stop" on their own are just words while you dictate. If that window has closed, or no text box is selected (the desktop, the taskbar, a list of files), the text goes on your clipboard instead, with a card to copy it.

## Good to know

- With **Listen for my wake word** off, the dictation key is ready about 15 seconds after Tessaya starts, so it does not slow the start; with it on, at once.
- **Clean up with AI**, first on the Dictation page, chooses how your words are tidied. **Claude** fixes misheard words, grammar and false starts through your Claude plan, and adds a second or two. **On this computer** fixes punctuation and filler words at once. **Off** types exactly what was heard. With Claude on, your words can be sent to it while you are still speaking, so the text is ready when you let go, even if you then discard it.
- **Writing style per app** lets chat apps stay casual and documents stay formal.
- **Recognition** is done on this computer by default, and nothing you say leaves it. On Windows it runs on your graphics card when one has room for the speech model, which is several times faster than the processor; **Use graphics card for recognition** under Dictation sets Auto, On (any card) or Off, and any problem on the card falls back to the processor without losing words. To use a cloud model instead, choose **Cloud** under Dictation, Recognition, pick OpenAI, Google Gemini or OpenRouter, and paste your own API key. Then your dictation and what you say to Tessaya are sent to that provider. The key is encrypted by your computer's account and never written to settings or logs. **Cloud model** lists the provider's models that can transcribe, with a recommended one, and **Test** sends a short sample clip (not your voice) and shows how long it took. If the cloud does not answer within a few seconds, this computer hears that sentence instead, so nothing you said is lost. Your languages are passed on as a hint when you speak one. Wake word checks always run on this computer, so audio from a false wake is never sent anywhere.
- **Several microphones:** if the one you chose hears nothing at all (a wireless receiver whose transmitter is off) while another of yours hears you, the dictation moves to that one with everything you said, and the pill says which. The next dictation starts on it too, until your chosen one hears you again. Set the order under Settings, Microphone, **If it hears nothing, use** and **Then**.
- It learns words from your edits; they are listed on the Dictation page.
- The last transcript is kept there in case a paste went to the wrong place. Dictated text never goes to the assistant as a command.
- On the phone there is no dictation into other apps (phones do not allow it); use the phone keyboard's own dictation.
